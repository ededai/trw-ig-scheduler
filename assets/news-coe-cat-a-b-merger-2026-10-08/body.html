LTA wants to merge Cat A and Cat B into one COE category for all cars, then add a rebate or a surcharge depending on how expensive the model is. The COE Cat A and Cat B merger is a proposal for now: public feedback opened on 8 October 2026 and closes on 2 November 2026 at 11.59pm, and the next bidding exercise runs under today's rules.

What LTA has proposed for the COE Cat A and Cat B merger

Cars currently bid in two pools. Cat A covers cars up to 1,600cc and 130bhp, plus EVs up to 110kW. Cat B covers everything above that. Under the proposal, both groups would bid in a single passenger car category and pay the same COE premium.

A "feebate" would then adjust what each buyer pays. LTA would take the median open market value (OMV) of each car model, rank those values, and cut the ranking into bands by percentile. Lower-value models get a rebate. Mid-range models pay the premium with no adjustment. Higher-value models pay a surcharge on top.

Two structures are on the table:

  • Three bands: a $15,000 rebate, a neutral band, and a $15,000 surcharge. LTA says this is simpler to run, though the jump between neighbouring bands is bigger.
  • Five bands: rebates of $15,000 or $7,500, a neutral band, and surcharges of $7,500 or $15,000.

In LTA's illustration, as reported by Mothership, a Toyota Sienta, Honda Freed or Volvo EX30 would sit in the rebate range. A Tesla Model 3, BYD Sealion 7 Performance or Mercedes-Benz A180 could land in surcharge territory. The bands would be reviewed every year, so a model could move as prices shift.

Why the two car categories stopped working

Cat A and Cat B were split so that buyers of bigger, pricier cars would not outbid families shopping for a mass-market car. The premiums no longer show that split.

At the 2nd bidding exercise of February 2026, Cat A closed at $106,501, which was $1,500 above Cat B at $105,001. LTA announced a review of the COE categories the following month. The gap has stayed thin since: in the 1st bidding exercise of October, which closed on 7 October 2026, Cat A finished at $130,001 and Cat B at $130,100.

That is a $99 difference.

When the premiums sit that close, engine size and power have stopped sorting buyers by budget. The feebate moves the price signal onto the value of the car itself. LTA has also said COE premiums will still be set by demand and supply. The review covers how cars are grouped; it sets no price target. What a merged COE could mean for your next car

Buyers of lower-value models could come out ahead. If a merged premium settled near today's Cat A level of $130,001, a $15,000 rebate would cut the entitlement cost by about 11.5%.

Buyers of higher-value models might pay more. The same $15,000 as a surcharge would take the entitlement past $145,000.

Where the merged premium itself settles is the open question. In the latest round, Cat A offered 1,232 certificates and Cat B offered 945. Pooling them puts every car buyer in one queue of about 2,177 certificates, and there is no track record yet for how bidding would behave in it.

What LTA has not decided yet

  • Whether to use three bands or five.
  • What happens to Cat E (Open), which can register any vehicle except motorcycles. It closed at $137,000 on 7 October.
  • Whether the feebate would also apply when you renew a COE.
  • A start date. LTA said it will share its findings and recommendations in the first half of 2027.

The renewal point matters most to owners near the 10-year mark. Renewal is paid at the prevailing quota premium (PQP) for your car's category, so a merged category, with or without a feebate, could change that bill. What to watch before 2 November

If you are buying a car soon, today's Cat A and Cat B rules apply to every bidding exercise until LTA announces otherwise, and the 2nd bidding exercise of October opens on 19 October. Latest premiums are on our COE results page.

To give your view, use LTA's feedback form at go.gov.sg/coe-pcd-feedback until 2 November 2026, 11.59pm. More than 200 members of the public, academics and industry representatives had already shared views before the paper came out.
