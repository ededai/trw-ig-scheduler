Detailed Findings

1. Suspected Odometer Adjustment HIGH

Based on our findings during the inspection, the overall wear and condition of the vehicle do not match the mileage shown on the odometer. We therefore suspect the mileage has been adjusted before. If so, the true mileage is likely higher than displayed, which affects the car's value and the expected remaining life of wear items (suspension, transmission, hybrid battery).

Recommendation: Ask the seller for full servicing records and cross-check the mileage history against past service invoices and inspection records. If the seller cannot provide consistent records, treat the displayed mileage as unreliable and value the car accordingly.

2. Rear Accident History — Not Properly Rectified HIGH

The rear of the vehicle shows clear signs of a previous accident. The repair was not adjusted back to the proper factory fitting — rear panels and the tailgate do not sit correctly. Rust is visible on the boot, indicating the repair was either incomplete or the protective coating was not restored, and moisture is getting in.

Left untreated, the rust will spread, and poor fitment can lead to water leaks into the boot, rattles and wind noise.

Recommendation: Have a body shop assess the rear structure (rear floor and chassis rails) before purchase. Budget for panel realignment and rust treatment. Ask the seller to disclose the accident and repair history.

3. Front Stabiliser Linkage Cracked HIGH

A crack was found on the front stabiliser (sway bar) link. This part controls body roll when cornering. A cracked link can fail completely, causing knocking noises over bumps, reduced stability and less predictable handling.

Recommendation: Replace before the car is driven regularly. We recommend replacing front stabiliser links as a pair for balanced handling. Safety-related — should be fixed by the seller or deducted from the price.

4. Clutch Plate Area Leaking MEDIUM

Leakage was found at the clutch plate area of the transmission. Leaks here can lead to clutch slipping, jerky gear changes and further damage to the clutch and transmission if not addressed. Transmission repairs on a hybrid can be costly.

Recommendation: Trace and fix the source of the leak (seal/gasket), then inspect the clutch pack for contamination and wear. Get a repair quotation before purchase and factor it into the price.

5. Rear Boot Stopper Missing LOW

The rear boot (tailgate) stopper is missing. Without it the tailgate does not seat properly, which can cause rattling, paint chipping and poor sealing. This may be related to the rear accident repair (Item 2).

Recommendation: Replace the stopper — a minor, low-cost fix. Check tailgate alignment at the same time.

Recommended Next Steps for the Buyer

  1. Request full service records and accident / insurance claim history from the seller to verify the mileage and rear repair.
  2. Obtain repair quotations for Items 2–5 and use them to renegotiate the price, or request the seller to rectify before handover.
  3. If the mileage cannot be verified, reconsider the purchase — an adjusted odometer is a serious concern for resale value and reliability.
  4. The Right Workshop can provide a detailed repair quotation for all items listed above on request.
